Dearest Ella,

I’ve been holding onto the words you sent me a few weeks ago, letting them settle in my heart while I waited for a day as special as today to tell you this: If my name feels euphonious to you, then your heart feels like home to me.

I remember you mentioning that "ache" you felt that night, and the pressure you feel to be "perfect" or keep things light. I need you to know—today and every day—that I don’t need a polished, plastic version of you. I love the version of you that is sentimental at midnight, the version that feels things deeply, and even the version that thinks she falls short. You aren’t a project to be perfected; you are a person to be known.

You spoke about existentialism—the idea that we create our own meaning. In this vast, uncertain universe, choosing you is the most "intentional" thing I have ever done. You aren't just a part of my day; you are the lens through which the whole world becomes worth looking at.

  • Thank you for choosing to be vulnerable instead of hiding.
  • Thank you for learning my language and making my world lighter.
  • Thank you for being the one who makes me want to show up, too.
  • And thank you for being the one to intentionally choose me every day, too.
You see me as the "coolest" or "smartest," but I’m just a person who got incredibly lucky to be found by you in that "unknown" place. You haven't just touched my heart; you've settled into it.

So on this Valentine's Day, don't worry about being "better" for me. Just keep being you. That is more than enough for a lifetime. I’ll see you soon.
